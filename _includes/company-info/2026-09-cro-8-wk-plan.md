# Audience

## Research Inputs
| Input | What it reveals | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stakeholder interviews | Business goals, known pain points, internal assumptions, constraints, priorities | Helps avoid generic recommendations and grounds the work in the company’s actual business context |
| Sales team interviews | Common buyer questions, objections, handoff issues, where prospects need explanation | Especially important for B2B/B2B2C commerce and sales-assisted journeys |
| Customer support interviews | Repeated confusion points, error patterns, account issues, support burden | Shows where the digital experience is failing to answer, reassure, or guide users |
| Customer/user interviews | What users are trying to do, what feels confusing, what creates doubt or confidence | Captures the emotional experience: clarity, trust, frustration, confidence, delight |
| Existing customer feedback | Reviews, survey responses, NPS comments, support tickets, chat logs, sales notes | Efficient way to uncover patterns without needing a large new research effort |
| Analytics review | Drop-off points, traffic patterns, conversion paths, form abandonment, completion rates | Identifies where users are leaving or failing to complete key actions |
| Funnel or journey data | Step-by-step movement through quote, purchase, application, signup, onboarding, or task flows | Helps connect experience friction to measurable business outcomes |
| Session recordings / heatmaps | Where users hesitate, rage click, miss information, scroll, backtrack, or abandon | Gives behavioral evidence behind the analytics |
| Experience walkthrough | Firsthand review of the journey from the user’s point of view | Surfaces clarity gaps, confusing hierarchy, trust issues, content problems, and interaction friction |
| Heuristic UX review | Usability issues against known UX principles and best practices | Adds expert analysis without relying only on anecdotal feedback |
| Content and messaging review | Whether users understand options, requirements, value, next steps, and consequences | Critical for complex decisions where confusion kills momentum |
| Competitive / comparator review | How similar companies explain, structure, or simplify comparable journeys | Provides useful reference points and inspiration, without blindly copying competitors |
| Internal process review | What happens before, during, and after the digital experience: sales follow-up, approvals, fulfillment, support | Important because friction often lives in the handoff between digital and human processes |
| Technical / platform constraints | CMS, ecommerce platform, CRM, analytics setup, dev capacity, data limitations | Keeps recommendations realistic and implementable |
| Business rules and edge cases | Eligibility rules, pricing logic, custom configurations, account types, permissions, approval steps | Especially important for complex commerce and workflow-heavy products |
| Existing personas or audience segments | Who the experience serves and how their needs differ | Helps avoid optimizing for a generic “user” when different audiences need different things |
| Current roadmap / planned changes | What the team is already planning to fix or redesign | Prevents duplicated work and helps prioritize recommendations that fit timing and strategy |

# 8 Week Plan

The plan should stay execution-focused: by the end of 8 weeks, the goal isn’t “perfect brand,” it’s a live offer, real outreach sent, conversations started, and a repeatable path toward the first paid audit.

| Phase | Weeks | Goal | Main Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Strategy & Positioning | 1–2 | Define the offer, target lanes, promise, pricing, and sales angle | Clear consulting offer + target client profile |
| 2. Sales Assets | 3–4 | Build the materials needed to make the offer credible and easy to buy | Landing page, sample audit/teardown, sales script, proposal template |
| 3. Outreach & Pipeline | 5–6 | Identify prospects and begin actual outreach | Prospect list, outreach messages, follow-up sequence, first conversations |
| 4. Sales Calls & Conversion | 7–8 | Turn interest into paid audit clients and refine based on feedback | Discovery calls, proposals, closing process, delivery workflow |

## Weekly
| Week | Focus | Deliverables |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Define the market and offer | Target lanes, ideal client profile, problem statement, offer promise |
| 2 | Package the audit | What’s included, what’s excluded, pricing, timeline, client outcomes |
| 3 | Build the landing page | Page outline, copy draft, CTA, proof/credibility section |
| 4 | Build sales support materials | Sample audit, discovery call script, proposal template, FAQ/objections |
| 5 | Build prospect list | 40–60 prospects across B2B commerce and application/signup flows |
| 6 | Start outreach | Send first outreach batch, follow-ups, track responses |
| 7 | Run sales conversations | Discovery calls, fit assessment, tailored proposals |
| 8 | Close and refine | Follow-up process, close first audit if possible, refine messaging based on market response |


### Week 1 - 9/7/2026
Week 1 goal: define the market, offer, and buying problem clearly enough that Weeks 2–4 can turn into pricing, landing page copy, and sales materials without wandering.
